13. TOPIC: GNN Discrepancy (59% original vs. 78% reproduction)
    * User Query: "...is this based on 6 class with 14 column no leakage as before you have given me when we trained 59% something how HANCONV got change..."
    * Explanation: The original GNN script omitted feature scaling (StandardScaler). In GNN message-passing, unscaled continuous inputs (e.g. age [12-17] vs. weight [100+ kg]) caused gradient vanishing/explosion, capping accuracy at 59.89%. Standardizing continuous inputs normalized node feature magnitudes, enabling the GNN to converge to 78.47% (single seed 79.15%).
    * Solution: Documented this controlled scale ablation and updated the GNN results text accordingly.

14. TOPIC: Feature Scaling Impact on Decision Trees vs. GNNs
    * User Query: "then on single seed if HANCONV can increase Accuracy then why not XGBOOST, and RF ?"
    * Explanation: Decision tree algorithms (XGBoost/RF) determine splits based on ordering (ordinal thresholding), which is invariant to monotonic transformations like scaling. GNNs are neural networks optimized via gradient descent; they require normalized inputs to prevent scaling mismatches in node embedding matrix multiplication.

1. FLAW 1: Evaluation Protocol Contradiction (5-Fold CV vs. 80/20 Holdout Split)
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: The manuscript contained a contradiction in the described evaluation protocol. Figure 2's TikZ diagram referenced "strictly on train folds --- zero cross-fold data contamination" implying K-fold cross-validation, while Section 4.7 and Section 5.11 explicitly described a single stratified 80/20 train/test holdout split.
   * Step-by-Step Solution:
     1. Located Figure 2 TikZ diagram source code in XGBOOST.tex (lines 603-608).
     2. Replaced K-fold/cross-fold references with holdout split terminology: "strictly on train split --- zero train/test data contamination".
     3. Located GNN cohort discussion in Section 6.2 (line 1734) and changed "test fold" to "test split" to align the terminology.
     4. Verified that all other sections (Abstract, Section 5.8, Section 6.4, Section 7.2) consistently refer to the "stratified 80/20 holdout split" with primary seed 42.

2. FLAW 2: Column Nomenclature Standardization ("14 columns" vs. "13 features" vs. "15 columns")
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: The text was inconsistent in referring to column and feature counts. It referenced "14 columns (excluding severity_score)", "15 columns", and "13 clinical features" in a confusing manner.
   * Explanation: The cleaned cohort dataset contains 15 core clinical columns (which includes 1 target label 'outc_cod' and 1 target-leakage column 'severity_score' which is discarded before training). Thus:
     - The raw cleaned schema contains 15 core clinical columns.
     - The leakage-prone column (severity_score) and target label (outc_cod) are excluded to yield 13 active features.
     - The model's predictive dataset contains 14 columns (13 features + 1 target outcome).
   * Step-by-Step Solution:
     1. Swept XGBOOST.tex for all feature and column count descriptions.
     2. Standardized the text in the Conclusion to read: "14-column leakage-free clinical dataset (comprising 13 clinical features and 1 target label)".
     3. Updated Figure 2 caption, Table 1 footnote, Table 2 description, and Section 6.4 to explicitly distinguish between the 15 columns in the database, the 13 clinical features used in training, and the 14-column predictive dataset.

3. FLAW 3: Imputation Mapping Completeness and CA Omission
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: The two-stage imputation pipeline was described as a "bijection" that resolves "approximately 100%" of outcome missingness. However, the imputation rule is a lookup mapping (modal outcome based on historical reaction term), not a bijection. Additionally, Congenital Anomaly (CA) was omitted from the lookup rules because no adverse event had CA as its modal outcome, meaning the lookup rule resolved only 95.09% of cases (with 4.91% falling back to Stage 2).
   * Step-by-Step Solution:
     1. Replaced the word "bijection" in Section 4.3 with "many-to-one deterministic lookup mapping" to ensure mathematical rigour.
     2. Added an inline footnote to Table 3 (Imputation Table) explaining the omission of CA: since no MedDRA Preferred Term had CA as its modal outcome in the training data, Stage 1 resolved 95.09% of cases, and the remaining 4.91% fell back to the Stage 2 clinical severity rule (where CA is correctly handled as a high-severity fallback).

4. FLAW 4: Dimensionality Ablation Accuracy Drop (pt_term Removal)
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: The ablation study showed a sharp 11-percentage-point drop in accuracy when moving from the 46-column analytical set to the 32-column configuration, but the manuscript failed to explain the cause.
   * Explanation: The 32-column configuration drops the high-cardinality reaction preferred term (pt_term) to test the predictive power of demographics and drug exposures alone.
   * Step-by-Step Solution:
     1. Updated Section 5.10 (Ablation study) in XGBOOST.tex to explicitly describe the cause: pt_term acts as a vital proxy for clinical pathology. Dropping it causes a sharp 11-point drop in accuracy, demonstrating that reaction terms carry the primary predictive signal, which is stabilized downstream by clinical fallback imputation.

5. FLAW 5: Table Footnote for Low-Support Categories (RI/DS)
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: Table 18 (now Table 14: tab:per_class_metrics) reported precision, recall, and F1-score for classes with very low support (e.g. RI had support=1, DS had support=1 in Diabetes Selected 4). Evaluating model comparisons on classes with test support < 5 is statistically unreliable and represents descriptive noise.
   * Step-by-Step Solution:
     1. Added a clear footnote to Table 14 (tab:per_class_metrics) caption: "*Note: Per-class metrics for classes with test support < 5 (RI, DS) are reported for completeness but should be interpreted descriptively only; they are not statistically reliable comparators between models."
     2. Adjusted the prose in Section 5.11.2 to frame RI and DS metrics strictly as descriptive observations, rather than asserting generalizable comparative superiority.

6. FLAW 6: Systematic Review Table Softening (Table 2 / tab:sysrev)
   * Reviewer's Question/Concern: The systematic review table (Table 2) showed checkmarks for "This Study" across all five evaluation criteria. Since the criteria were selected by the authors based on gaps they sought to fill, the checkmark table is self-referential and visually oversells the novelty.

     1. Kept the prose caveat in Section 2.2 stating that the comparative scoring represents literature coverage rather than an independent benchmark.

   * Step-by-Step Solution:

     3. Updated the Conclusion to state: "establishing a preliminary sample-size boundary for architectural recommendations that remains qualified by asymmetric class-imbalance tuning between models."
